## Modules

`server/modules/teams` (teams, invitations, team registration), `submissions` (drafts, versions, files, read access), `judging` (criteria, assignment, scorecards, pure `scoring.ts`), `results` (standings, publication, public and own views), `certificates` (templates, issuing, verification, PDF), and `server/storage` (storage interface, local driver, upload validation). Pages call these services; route handlers contain no database or file-system code (enforced by `tests/architecture.test.ts`).

## Teams and event registration modes

An event is **individual** or **team** (`registrationMode`, with min and max team size; set when the event is created and locked once teams or registrations exist). Individual registration is refused on team events and team creation on individual events.

- **Membership.** One team per person per event is a database guarantee: `team_members.event_id` with a unique `(event_id, user_id)` and a composite foreign key to `(team_id, event_id)`. The maximum team size is enforced by a trigger that locks the team row, so two simultaneous joins cannot take the last place. Exactly one leader per team (existing partial unique index).
- **Invitations.** A leader invites an email address; the invitation stores only the SHA-256 of a 256-bit token, expires after 7 days, and one open invitation per team and address is guaranteed by a partial unique index. The emailed link only opens a page; **only an explicit POST answers** (mail scanners cannot accept or decline). Only the person whose verified email matches can preview or answer. The answer is a conditional update (`PENDING`, unexpired), so exactly one request wins; accepting and joining are one transaction (a full team or an existing membership rolls the whole answer back and leaves the invitation open). The invitation response never reveals whether an address has an account.
- **Leaving and removal.** Members leave; the leader removes members or disbands. A registered team cannot drop below the event's minimum without cancelling its registration. Disbanding is refused once a project was submitted.
- **Team registration.** The leader registers the team; every member gets a registration row with the same status. **Capacity counts teams**; a team is confirmed or waitlisted as one unit and the oldest waiting team is promoted first. All seat changes run under the event row lock, so simultaneous registrations never exceed capacity (tested with 7 teams for 3 places). Someone joining an already registered team is registered with it.

## Submissions

A confirmed registration (for team events: through a registered team) is required. One submission per individual or team; every team member edits the same one. `DRAFT` (private) becomes `SUBMITTED` on submit; **every submit or resubmit and every file change of a submitted project records an immutable version** (a JSON snapshot of the text, links and file list, who, when). Deadline: `submissionDeadline`, otherwise the end of the event; after it nothing can change. Once a judge starts scoring (`UNDER_REVIEW`) or the submission is `DISQUALIFIED`, it is locked. A database CHECK makes "has a submission time" equivalent to "not a draft". Disqualification by organizers is recorded with its reason.

## Storage

`Storage` (`put`, `get`, `remove`) is the only way application code reaches files. The `local` driver writes under `STORAGE_LOCAL_DIR` (git-ignored); it refuses any key that is not a short relative path of `[a-z0-9-]` segments, re-checks that the resolved path stays inside the root, and never overwrites. Keys are generated by the server. **`STORAGE_DRIVER=s3` is not implemented**: it fails loudly at first use rather than silently storing somewhere else. An S3-compatible driver is a separate piece of work (documented in the backlog); no paid provider is used.

Upload validation (`file-validation.ts`, pure and exhaustively tested): allow-listed extension **and** matching declared MIME type **and** magic bytes (UTF-8 text check for `txt`/`md`), 10 MB per file, 5 files and 25 MB per submission, sanitized display name. Downloads are authorized per request and served as attachments with `nosniff`, a sandboxing CSP and `no-store`. A ZIP is stored as an opaque private file and is never opened or extracted by the server; organizers and judges should treat downloaded archives like any untrusted file (**no malware scanning exists**).

## Judging

- **Assignment** is per event by an organizer (existing, confirmed accounts only) and records an event-scoped `JUDGE` role. A participant of the event cannot be assigned. The assignment (not a `JUDGE` role elsewhere) is what authorizes scoring.
- **Blind and conflict-free.** Judges see submitted work without author or team names and never their own project; a judge who is also an author is refused (`conflict_of_interest`).
- **Timing.** Scoring opens at `judgingStartsAt`, or otherwise after the submission deadline, and closes at `judgingEndsAt` when set; it stops when results are published.
- **Scorecards.** A scorecard is one judge's scores for one submission, saved under an advisory lock per (submission, judge). Scores are 0 to the criterion's maximum with at most two decimals (validated in the service and again by the existing database trigger and CHECK). A draft can be edited; **finalizing requires every criterion**; a final scorecard is frozen until an organizer reopens it with a reason. Criteria (name, maximum, weight) cannot change after scoring starts. Every save, finalize and reopen is audited.

### The scoring formula (also in `judging/scoring.ts`)

For criterion *c* with maximum *M<sub>c</sub>* and weight *W<sub>c</sub>*, and a judge's score *s<sub>c</sub>*:

```text
judge percentage   P = 100 * sum(W_c * s_c / M_c) / sum(W_c)
final score        F = mean of P over ALL assigned judges (each with a finalized, complete scorecard)
```

Intermediate values are not rounded; the stored and displayed numbers are rounded to 3 decimals. Normalizing by the maximum first means a 0-10 criterion and a 0-100 criterion weigh exactly as their weights say. Example: criteria Innovation (max 10, weight 2) and Impact (max 100, weight 1); scores 8 and 50 give `100 * (2*0.8 + 1*0.5) / 3 = 70`.

## Results

Standings come only from **finalized** scorecards. A submission missing a finalized card from any assigned judge is **not ranked** and listed as missing; nothing is estimated. **Publishing is refused unless criteria, judges and submissions exist and nothing is missing**, and the standings are recomputed inside the publishing transaction. Ranking is "competition ranking" with ties (1, 2, 2, 4) and no hidden tie-breaker. Awards come from rank only: 1 winner, 2 runner-up, 3 to 5 finalist. Disqualified submissions are excluded. Draft results are stored (with a per-judge and per-criterion breakdown for organizers); published results are public; unpublishing needs a reason and is blocked while valid certificates exist. Publication, unpublication and calculation are audited (publication with a snapshot of the top results).

Visibility: the public leaderboard shows rank, tie marker, award, score, project title and the team name (or the participant's name for individual events). It never shows judge identities, per-judge scores, feedback or emails. A participant or team sees its own result with per-criterion averages and anonymous feedback. Unpublished results answer `404` exactly like a nonexistent page.

## Certificates

Issued by organizers only from published results, one per person per event, of the highest type earned (winner, finalist, participation for others who submitted); team results go to every confirmed member; disqualified submissions earn none; repeating is safe and a revoked certificate is never reissued silently. Each has a unique unguessable code `ST-XXXXX-XXXXX-XXXXX` (75 bits). Wording comes from a per-event, per-type **template** (heading, text with only `{{name}}`, `{{event}}`, `{{team}}`, `{{project}}`, `{{rank}}`, signatory) or a built-in default. The PDF is generated on demand with pdf-lib (A4 landscape, black on white, the verification URL printed on it, fixed creation date) for the recipient (while valid) and the event's organizers. **Public verification** (`/verify/certificate/{code}`) confirms genuineness, recipient name, event, type, issue date and revocation, nothing else. Revocation needs a reason, is audited, and shows on the verification page and stops the recipient's download.

## Known limitations

- Certificates use the standard PDF fonts (Western European characters). Other characters in a name print as `?` instead of failing; embedding a Unicode font is open work.
- S3-compatible storage is not implemented; local disk only. No virus or content scanning of uploads.
- Team capacity is a number of teams; the organizer tables count people.
- Tie-breaking beyond equal scores is not offered; ties stay ties.
- Event times are still UTC-only. No emails are sent when a team registration is promoted from the waiting list, when judging opens, or when results are published.
- Certificate type is derived from rank (1, 2 to 5, others); organizers cannot set custom awards.
- A judge removed from an event after scoring cannot be removed (their scores would be orphaned).
- Self-reviewed only; an independent security review is still recommended before real user data.
